---
title: "Don’t try on thoughts"
date: 2026-09-06
---

There are many reasons to write by hand, instead of using AI. A classic reason is that [writing is thinking](https://www.paulgraham.com/writes.html) — in other words that if you're not writing, you're not clearly thinking. 

But here's another reason: **when you write with AI, you're trying on thoughts and seeing what fits "close enough"**. 

You give AI a jumble of thoughts, and ask it to polish and put things together. Since [all transformations of text are lossy](https://sophiebits.com/2026/06/25/there-are-no-lossless-transformations-of-natural-language-text), some things change. A tone shift here, an idea inserted there, an emphasis smoothed out. 

You read it and you don't like it. You ask for another draft, a bit more like _this_. You and AI go back and forth, until you reach something that conveys your idea. 

But it's _not_ the same as your idea. Much like a clothing store, you went to the Thoughts Store and tried on thoughts in the fitting room until one felt good enough — good enough tonally, good enough emotionally, good enough substantively. 

But thoughts and emotions are not like clothes. You have the full range of human expression at your fingertips. You don't have to — and shouldn't — settle for an expression that seems like a _close enough_ approximation to what you originally meant. 

I want to read your tailor-made thoughts, put into writing for a reader. Not something off the rack.
